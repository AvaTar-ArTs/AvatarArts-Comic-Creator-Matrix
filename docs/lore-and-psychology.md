# Lore, Psychology, and Curse Design

## Three identities

Every major character has a public identity, a private identity, and a mythic identity. Story pressure comes from forcing the three to disagree in the same scene.

## Crimson Curse Master

- Public identity: feared poisoner, necromancer, and keeper of forbidden remedies.
- Private identity: meticulous and lonely, afraid he is not exceptional enough to be remembered.
- Buried truth: the curse is not an external power; it is the most honest version of him.
- Desire: to be chosen without having to ask.
- Wound: rejection and insignificance.
- Fatal pattern: engineers situations that make choice appear inevitable.
- Moral rule: never use a curse for an ordinary task.
- Rule violation: he breaks that rule for someone he loves, proving the curse has learned intimacy.

## Curse as behavioral prison

A curse is not a costume or a spell effect. It is a behavioral prison with a reward, a demand, and a price.

Each curse records: hunger, personality, visual anchor, forced behavior, price, lost memory, preferred host, transformation, failure mode, and relationship to the bearer.

The seven curses are unreliable. Records claim seven. Mirrors, erased pages, and contradictory witnesses imply an eighth. Choosing a curse does not mean controlling which part of the self it activates.

## Visual state palette

| State | Palette | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Self-control | Black-burgundy | Contained danger |
| Active choice | Scarlet | Agency with cost |
| External influence | Poison green | Contamination or persuasion |
| Corpse-memory | Bone white | Knowledge without life |
| Suppressed truth | Bruised violet | Denial becoming visible |
| False salvation | Sickly gold | Relief that asks for obedience |
| Irreversible consequence | Blue-black | The new permanent rule |

## Story progression

Use this sequence for a curse-centered arc: procedure → anomaly → investigation → recognition → false control → moral choice → curse-specific consequence → new permanent rule.

A lore reveal is successful only when it changes a previous image, relationship, or decision.
