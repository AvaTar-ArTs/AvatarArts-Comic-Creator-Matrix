# Agent and Skill Orchestration

## Pipeline

```text
Creative Producer
→ The Spark
→ The Cartographer
→ The Canonist
→ The Cursewright
→ The Dramaturge
→ The Panelwright
→ The Forge
→ The Letter-Witch
→ The Red Pen
→ The Gatekeeper
→ The Archivist
```

Agents may branch during exploration, but production should converge to one declared canon and one active format.

## Responsibilities

### Creative Producer

Extracts audience, purpose, medium, emotional response, constraints, references, and unknowns.

### The Spark

Generates meaningfully different directions across genre, era, culture, mythology, medium, and visual language.

### The Cartographer

Turns research into actionable design rules, not an inspiration dump.

### The Canonist

Locks identity anchors, world laws, recurring motifs, contradictions, and provenance.

### The Cursewright

Defines powers, prices, behavioral constraints, failure states, inheritance, and irreversible consequences.

### The Dramaturge

Maps desire, fear, defense mechanisms, shame, obsession, agency, denial, and transformation.

### The Panelwright

Converts beats into panels, camera distance, panel geometry, gutters, page turns, scroll timing, and reading order.

### The Forge

Generates reference sheets, panels, key art, variations, transitions, and short motion sequences.

### The Letter-Witch

Assigns type behavior to each voice, artifact, institution, curse, and sound effect.

### The Red Pen

Runs separate visual, story, canon, and audience critiques.

### The Gatekeeper

Checks readability, continuity, format, export quality, licensing, and release readiness.

### The Archivist

Stores prompts, references, seeds, model/provider, versions, hashes, tags, relationships, and cross-media appearances.

## Brainstorming protocol

### Clarify

Define outcome, audience, format, emotional target, references, restrictions, and unknowns.

### Expand

Branch across character nature, mythology, setting, genre, visual grammar, typography, motion, and publishing context.

### Challenge

Ask:

- Is this generic?
- Is it merely decorative?
- What is the contradiction?
- What is the cost?
- What can the character not control?
- What would make this impossible to confuse with another property?

### Select

Score each concept for identity, originality, story yield, visual yield, emotional yield, production yield, and cross-media yield.

### Commit

Record the selected direction, rejected alternatives, canon rules, and next artifact.

## Three-critic gate

Every major output should receive:

1. **Visual critique** — Is it compelling and legible?
2. **Story critique** — Does it communicate progression and change?
3. **Canon critique** — Does it belong to this world and preserve continuity?

Then run reader simulation:

- What do you notice first?
- What happened?
- What question remains?
- What do you expect next?
- Which symbol do you remember?
