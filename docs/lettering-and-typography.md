# Lettering, Typography, and Voice System

Lettering is treated as acting. Every voice has a shape, spacing, pressure, and relationship to the panel.

## Font cast

| Voice | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Main dialogue | Humanist comic face, readable, restrained balloon shapes |
| Narration | Narrow serif or editorial face, measured line length |
| Archive | Rigid small caps, aligned like a bureaucratic record |
| Curse | Letterforms slightly compressed, red ink intrusion, controlled repetition |
| Punk graffiti | Handwritten, uneven baseline, selective overprint |
| Corpse-memory | Faint captions, missing punctuation, broken cadence |
| Scientific note | Monospaced, precise, diagram-adjacent |
| Relic | Decorative display face used rarely, never for exposition |
| Sound effect | Drawn into the environment, not pasted on top |

## Crimson Curse Master voices

- Cursemaster: narrow, restrained, exact; the more frightened he is, the more grammatical he becomes.
- Official archive: rigid small caps with redactions and inconsistent dates.
- Dead memory: faint captions that lose punctuation as identity decays.
- Poison: thin text that spreads across a surface or escapes the balloon.
- Hunger: oversized words with missing letters, as if something has eaten the sentence.
- Quiet Crown: annotations in margins; no conventional balloons.
- Hollow Skin: imitates the voice of the person being impersonated.
- Unchosen curse: appears only in gutters and page margins until selected.

## Composition rules

- Balloon tails must reveal who is speaking without requiring a label.
- Text must never cover the only evidence needed to understand a panel.
- Use captions for distance, balloons for presence, and environmental text for world authority.
- Let silence be a designed element: an empty balloon, a cut-off sentence, or a panel with no text.
- Keep mobile lettering legible at thumbnail scale before adding decorative distortion.
